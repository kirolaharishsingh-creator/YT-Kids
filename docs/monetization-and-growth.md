# Monetization & Growth Plan

Numbers below are current as of Sept 2026 (sources at bottom). Revisit this doc
periodically — YouTube changes these thresholds.

**Format change (Sept 2026):** the channel switched from vertical 9:16 Shorts
to **16:9 landscape**, still ~15-20 second videos. This is a significant
strategic shift from what this doc originally assumed — landscape videos
generally don't qualify for YouTube's Shorts shelf regardless of duration, so
the whole monetization path below changed with it. Read the "Reality check"
section carefully before treating the old Shorts-based numbers (if you've
seen them elsewhere) as still relevant — they aren't.

## Reality check first — this is the hard part now

With 16:9 landscape, these videos are **regular YouTube uploads**, not
Shorts. That has one serious consequence: YouTube Partner Program's
watch-hour threshold (see below) is measured in **total watch time
accumulated across all views**, and a 15-20 second video contributes almost
nothing per view compared to longer content.

**The math, worked out honestly:**
- Threshold: **4,000 watch hours in 12 months** (rising to 8,000 hours from
  Feb 2027 — see below).
- 4,000 hours = 14,400,000 seconds of total watch time needed.
- Even assuming a generous 15 seconds of average watch time per view (i.e.
  near-full completion, which is optimistic), that's **~960,000 views**
  just to clear the hour threshold — before any monetization starts.
- Realistic average view duration is usually lower than full completion, so
  the true number is likely higher, possibly by a lot.

Compare that to the Shorts-specific path this doc originally assumed (10M
Shorts views in 90 days) — that was actually the *easier* threshold for
short-duration content, specifically because YouTube built it to work with
looped/short view patterns. Landscape short clips get none of that
accommodation and instead compete on the same watch-hour math as long-form
content, without long-form's duration to earn hours quickly.

**Second compounding problem — discovery, not just monetization:** YouTube's
regular-video algorithm favors session watch time. A 15-20s landscape video
gives it almost nothing to work with compared to even a 3-5 minute video,
so distribution/recommendation is also harder, independent of the
monetization threshold.

**What this means practically:** treat the ~960k-view watch-hour threshold
as the realistic bar to clear before ad revenue starts, and expect the path
there to be meaningfully slower and harder than the Shorts path would have
been. This isn't a reason not to proceed — just an honest recalibration of
the timeline and effort required.

## YouTube Partner Program thresholds (standard path — applies now)

| Tier | Subscribers | Watch requirement | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | 500 | 3,000 public watch hours (12 months) OR 3M Shorts views (90 days, N/A for this channel) | Fan funding (Super Thanks, etc.) |
| Tier 2 | 1,000 | 4,000 public watch hours (12 months) OR 10M Shorts views (90 days, N/A for this channel) | Ad revenue sharing |

**Change coming:** from Feb 1, 2027, new applicants need **8,000 watch
hours** (up from 4,000). Reaching Tier 2 before that date is meaningfully
easier than after — worth prioritizing speed now while the lower threshold
still applies.

## Expected earnings once monetized

- **Landscape/regular-video RPM is much better than Shorts RPM** — roughly
  **$5–15 per 1,000 views** for general-audience content (vs. Shorts'
  $0.01–0.08). This is the upside of the format switch: once past the much
  harder threshold above, each view is worth dramatically more.
- A video with 1M views at this RPM ≈ **$5,000–$15,000** — a completely
  different scale than the Shorts math this doc used to cite.
- **Avoid the "Made for Kids" designation if at all possible.** It disables
  comments, personalization, memberships, and Super Chat/Thanks, and cuts RPM
  to roughly **$1–3** (vs the $5-15 above) because only contextual
  (non-personalized) ads are allowed. This is a strong reason the channel is
  framed as general/family folk-tale content, not toddler-targeted — though
  note YouTube's system can still auto-flag content as kid-directed based on
  characters/tone/language regardless of your setting, so keep the tone,
  narration, and thumbnails aimed broadly (family/general audience) rather
  than toddler-coded.

## Revenue beyond ad share

- Cross-posting the same clips to Instagram Reels / TikTok / YouTube Shorts
  itself (a landscape video can still be *cross-posted* as a Shorts-shaped
  crop on those platforms, even if the main upload doesn't get Shorts
  placement) — this is worth considering as a parallel distribution channel
  since it doesn't cost extra production, just an export step.
- Sponsorships once subscriber count and engagement are real.
- Compilation videos (stringing multiple fables into one longer upload) —
  actually a stronger lever now than it was under the Shorts model, since it
  directly attacks the watch-hour problem: a 5-minute compilation of fifteen
  20-second fables earns hours ~15x faster per view than a single short
  upload would.

## Realistic path to the watch-hour threshold

- Post consistently — daily or near-daily — same discovery logic as before,
  though the regular algorithm's ranking signals differ from Shorts (session
  watch time, click-through, and audience retention curve all matter more
  than raw post frequency).
- **Seriously consider compilation videos** as a core part of the growth
  strategy, not just a nice-to-have — see above. This may end up being the
  single biggest lever for reaching the watch-hour threshold in a reasonable
  timeframe.
- Realistic timeline to hit the monetization threshold: **materially longer
  than the 3-6 months estimated under the old Shorts plan** — budget for
  6-12+ months minimum, given the harder watch-hour math above. This should
  be treated as a real planning input, not just caution.

## Action checklist (things only you can do)

- [ ] Create the YouTube channel (channel name, handle, profile art)
- [ ] Set channel default audience setting thoughtfully — do not blanket-mark
      "Made for Kids" unless you specifically intend toddler/YouTube Kids app
      placement (see tradeoff above)
- [ ] Turn on 2-Step Verification on the Google account (required for YPP)
- [ ] Link an AdSense account (needed before ad revenue can ever pay out —
      can be done any time, doesn't require being monetized yet)
- [ ] Post consistently and track views/subscribers/watch-hours weekly —
      watch-hours specifically, since that's the actual threshold now
- [ ] Decide early whether compilation videos are part of the plan — this
      changes production planning (how episodes get grouped/edited together)
- [ ] Apply for YPP as soon as thresholds are hit — don't wait, especially
      given the Feb 2027 threshold increase
