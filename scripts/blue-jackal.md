# Script: The Blue Jackal

**Format:** ~18-20 seconds · vertical 9:16 · Panchatantra fable series
**Status:** ✅ fact-checked (see `research/blue-jackal/notes.md`) — ready
for shot-list/production

---

## Beat 1 — Hook (0:00–0:03) — Mascot

**Visual:** The peacock narrator mascot, perched on a branch, addresses camera.
**On-screen text:** "The Blue Jackal 🔵"
**Narration (VO):**
> "What would you do if turning blue made you king?"

---

## Beat 2 — Setup (0:03–0:08) — In-scene

**Visual:** A jackal, fleeing, tumbles into a dyer's vat and emerges a
strange, vivid blue. A small group of forest animals — deer, a monkey, a
bird — gather around him, wide-eyed and awed.
**Narration (VO):**
> "A jackal fell into a vat of blue dye — and the forest, having never
> seen such a color, crowned him their king."

---

## Beat 3 — Turn (0:08–0:13) — In-scene

**Visual:** The blue jackal seated on a raised rock or mound like a throne,
forest animals bowing/attending him, living in comfort and authority.
**Narration (VO):**
> "He ruled in style — until the night the jackals began to howl."

---

## Beat 4 — Payoff (0:13–0:17) — In-scene

**Visual:** Night. The blue jackal, ears perked at the distant howling,
loses control, throws his head back, and howls along with the ordinary
jackals in the background. The forest animals nearby react with dawning
realization.
**Narration (VO):**
> "He couldn't help himself — he howled right back, and his secret was
> out."

---

## Beat 5 — Moral (0:17–0:20) — Mascot

**Visual:** Back to the peacock mascot, warm expression.
**On-screen text:** "Moral: You can't hide what you truly are."
**Narration (VO):**
> "Moral of the story? You can dye your fur, but you can't hide your
> true nature."

---

## Production notes

- **Mascot shots (Beats 1 & 5) are fully reused from fable #1 — no new
  generation needed.** Same generic-talking clips as fable #2
  (`6a35ec14-57c1-4bba-8243-7e8be5f48e61` for the hook, `3abdf454-7684-460a-a2d2-7af777ae1fca`
  for the moral) — this fable's hook/moral audio gets placed under the
  existing footage in editing.
- Every in-scene visual prompt must append the fixed style fragment from
  `character-bible/style-guide.md` (fairy tale illustration style, warm
  jewel-toned palette, no photorealism) plus the anti-split-screen/
  anti-panel language and individual-pose guidance documented in
  `video-projects/01-lion-and-the-rabbit/shot-2-iteration-log.md` and
  `shot-3-iteration-log.md`.
- A fable-specific character reference sheet (blue jackal + a couple of
  forest animal "subjects") should be generated and locked first, same
  process as fable #1's `character-reference-prompt.md`.
- Location: single forest-clearing setting throughout (no second location
  needed, unlike fable #1's well or fable #2's palace) — use the locked
  `Neelvan-Forest` Element (id `e59c0df0-180e-4d97-9ca1-4ac2a550ef36`) for
  consistency.
- Beat 4's ordinary jackals (the howling chorus) can be background/
  silhouette figures — they don't need the same design consistency as the
  blue jackal himself, since they're not recurring named characters.
- **Voice: single-narrator, confirmed channel-wide.** The mascot/
  storyteller voice reads the entire narration; no other character gets a
  distinct voice or lip-sync — same Isla voice as every prior fable.
- **Length discipline:** fable #1's voiceover overran its target (33.7s
  vs. the intended 15-20s), which significantly increased the animation
  budget. This script's narration lines are kept deliberately short/tight
  to land closer to the true 18-20s target — worth confirming actual
  voiceover duration against this before committing to a full animation
  budget.
