# Script: The Musical Donkey

**Format:** ~18-20 seconds · vertical 9:16 · Panchatantra fable series
**Status:** ✅ fact-checked (see `research/musical-donkey/notes.md`) —
ready for shot-list/production

---

## Beat 1 — Hook (0:00–0:03) — Mascot

**Visual:** The peacock narrator mascot, perched on a branch, addresses camera.
**On-screen text:** "The Musical Donkey 🎶🫏"
**Narration (VO):**
> "What happens when a donkey just... can't resist a solo?"

---

## Beat 2 — Setup (0:03–0:08) — In-scene

**Visual:** A donkey and a jackal sneaking through a moonlit vegetable
field at night, cheeks full, having a great time. The donkey stops,
lifts his head toward the moon, delighted.
**Narration (VO):**
> "A donkey and a jackal raided farms together every night — until one
> night, the donkey decided he simply had to sing."

---

## Beat 3 — Turn (0:08–0:12) — In-scene

**Visual:** The jackal urgently gesturing for quiet, alarmed. The donkey,
ignoring him, throws his head back mid-bray, eyes closed in blissful
performance.
**Narration (VO):**
> "The jackal begged him to stay quiet — but the donkey threw back his
> head and brayed as loud as he could."

---

## Beat 4 — Payoff (0:12–0:17) — In-scene

**Visual:** Farmhouse lights snapping on in the background. Farmers with
sticks rushing toward the field. Cut to the donkey running off with a
wooden mortar now tied around his neck, dejected. **No depiction of the
beating itself** — imply the chase/commotion, cut straight to the
after-the-fact consequence (the mortar, the sulk), same non-graphic
treatment as prior fables.
**Narration (VO):**
> "The farmers came running — and the donkey learned his lesson the hard
> way."

---

## Beat 5 — Moral (0:17–0:20) — Mascot

**Visual:** Back to the peacock mascot, warm expression.
**On-screen text:** "Moral: Even talent has a time and place."
**Narration (VO):**
> "Moral of the story? Even talent has a time and place."

---

## Production notes

- **Mascot shots (Beats 1 & 5) are fully reused from fable #1 — no new
  generation needed.** Same generic-talking clips as fables #2-4
  (`6a35ec14-57c1-4bba-8243-7e8be5f48e61` for the hook,
  `3abdf454-7684-460a-a2d2-7af777ae1fca` for the moral).
- Every in-scene visual prompt must append the fixed style fragment from
  `character-bible/style-guide.md`, plus the anti-split-screen/anti-panel
  language and individual-pose guidance from
  `video-projects/01-lion-and-the-rabbit/shot-2-iteration-log.md` and
  `shot-3-iteration-log.md`.
- **Content safety:** the farmers beat the donkey with sticks in the
  traditional telling — do not depict this. Cut from "farmers running in"
  directly to the aftermath (donkey trudging away with the mortar around
  his neck), the same way fable #1 skipped from "leapt in" straight to
  ripples, and fable #4 skips from "opened his mouth" straight to the
  stick falling with no visible impact.
- A fable-specific character reference sheet (donkey + jackal) should be
  generated and locked first, same process as fable #1's
  `character-reference-prompt.md`.
- Location: a moonlit farm/vegetable field at night — new territory, not
  yet part of the locked world-bible (similar situation to fable #4's
  village). Design consistent with the established art style but expect
  this to need its own small reference/lock pass.
- **Voice: single-narrator, confirmed channel-wide.** Same Isla voice as
  every prior fable.
- **Length discipline:** keep narration tight for an 18-20s target,
  following fables #3-4's approach rather than fable #1's 33.7s overrun.
