# Research: The Musical Donkey

## Source

From the **Panchatantra** (Book 5) — a classic cautionary fable, widely
retold in Indian children's story collections.

✅ **Fact-checked 2026-09-16.** Cross-referenced via web search against
multiple independent, converging retellings (vedantu.com, tell-a-tale.com,
kidsgen.com, culturalindia.net, talesofpanchatantra.com,
mokshamelodies.in, hubpages.com, firstcry.com, india-a2z.com — nine
independent sources; a Wikisource primary-translation page was also listed
but not fetched directly, corroborated via the others). Core facts
confirmed:
- A donkey and a jackal were friends who **raided farms together at
  night**, stealing vegetables
- One night, full of the meal and the moonlight, the donkey insisted on
  **singing** (braying) — lifting his head to the moon
- The jackal **warned him repeatedly** that the noise would wake the
  farmers and give them away, and tried to get him to stay quiet
- The donkey brayed anyway; the jackal **fled and hid**, escaping
  unharmed
- The farmers woke, found the donkey in their field, and **chased and
  beat him** with sticks — then tied a **wooden mortar around his neck**
  before releasing him (a fitting, almost comic punishment: now he's
  slower and more burdened, same as his vanity slowed him down)

## Moral

**Vanity/pride invites danger — know the right time and place, even for
something you're good at.** (Commonly phrased: "think before you act" or
"even good friends' advice is wasted on someone who won't listen.")

## Content-safety note

The donkey is beaten by farmers (with sticks) — needs the same non-graphic
treatment as prior fables' physical consequences: imply the chase/
commotion, don't depict the beating itself in detail. Less severe than
fable #4's traditional death, but still worth explicit care.

## Why this fable for video #5

- Lighter, more comedic tone than #2 (mercy) or #4 (tortoise's fall) —
  good tonal variety, closer to #1's "consequences of not listening"
  flavor but with a friend-to-friend warning dynamic instead of a
  stranger's trick.
- Strong visual hook: a donkey singing to the moon is an immediately fun,
  silly image — good for thumbnail appeal.
- Compact cast: donkey, jackal, and a farmer or two for the chase beat —
  similar scale to prior fables.
